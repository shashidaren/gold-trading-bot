## 1. THE FINDING — the +0.30R ratchet is where the win rate goes to die

`BE_TRIGGER_R = 0.30` means the stop jumps to entry after **0.60·ATR** of
favourable move — on this feed ≈ $1.2 on a $4,300 market, i.e. one 1-min bar of
noise. The consequences are all in the timing table:

| Outcome | n | median hold | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TP | 14 | 10.2 min | 31.9 | 2944 |
| SL | 58 | 4.0 min | 14.0 | 35.0 |
| **BE scratch** | **92** | **1.5 min** | 7.0 | **14.0** |

A scratch is decided in 90 seconds. It is not a trade outcome, it is the noise
band being harvested. 92 of 119 new-regime trades (77%) arm the ratchet within
~2 minutes and are then refunded to $0 — and because `log_trade` writes the
*live* stop, **6 of our 14 wins also log `Stop_Loss == Entry_Price`** (#95,
#120, #123, #133, #156, #157): they armed, held, and still reached TP.

Reading: 0.75R is the knee. Below it the ratchet eats runs it does not protect;
above ~1R it stops doing much of anything (the trade is already at +1R of a
1.5R target). **Adopted this cycle: `BE_TRIGGER_R` 0.30 → 0.75** (§7).

Honest caveats, all of them pointing the same way (the effect is real, the
magnitude is not):
- One regime, 9 trading days, n=119 — and 09-14 alone contributes +$176 of the
  +$222 scratch-replay figure, with 09-13 (Sunday) at −$15.50.
- Cascade-ignorance cuts both ways: without the ratchet the book is busy for
  hours, so it takes ~40–60 trades instead of 119. Trade *count* and
  per-day bleed are not comparable across rows; expectancy-per-trade is.
- Real fills add spread/slippage (~0.05–0.11/trade here) which the ratchet
  never paid.
- 09-10 PM's counterfactual on 13 scratches said "BE ≈ P&L-neutral" (saved
  $30 of SLs, killed $34 of TPs). With 92 scratches the trade-off is no longer
  neutral — the killed runs now outweigh the saved stops by ~7:1 in dollars.
  The 13-trade sample was simply too small to see it.
- **Watch for the failure mode this creates:** with a 0.75R trigger a trade can
  now give back 0.75R before the scratch, so per-trade tail risk rises
  (worst-case ≈ −1R still, but more trades will reach it instead of being
  refunded). Judge the next cycle on **P&L and bleed-per-day**, not on win rate
  alone.

## 3. Frequency: the bot is now a scratch machine that re-loads instantly

Trades/day: 11, 6, 8, 15, 23, 32, 5, **62**, 2. On 09-14 the book entered 9
times in 43 minutes (08:05–08:48, #124–#132, every one a scratch).

Mechanism: `get_consecutive_sl_count` breaks on TP and counts SL, so **a BE exit
neither triggers nor blocks the cooldown** — after a 1.5-minute scratch the next
signal is instantly eligible, usually the same dying setup on the same bar.
86 of 164 entries (52%) came within 10 minutes of the previous exit and went
60 BE / 21 SL / 5 TP for −$43.19.

Two consequences:
1. **In simulation this churn is free. In reality it is not:** 164 trades ×
   0.05–0.11 spread ≈ $8–18, i.e. 6–13% of the whole drawdown, and 09-14 alone
   would have paid ~$3–7 to scratch 44 times. Any future LIVE-mode decision must
   price this in.
2. It inflates the sample size without adding information — 119 "trades" in the
   new regime is really ~35 independent decisions once scratches of the same
   move are collapsed. Judge strategy changes on distinct setups, not row counts.

Raising `BE_TRIGGER_R` to 0.75 fixes most of this by itself (fewer scratches →
fewer instant re-entries: the cascade-aware replay takes 59 of 119 signals at
0.75R). If the next drop still shows sub-10-minute re-entries dominating, the
queued candidates to consider are **a short post-scratch pause (~5 min)** and
**"BE resets the SL streak"** (candidate b) — note they pull in opposite
directions: one tightens, one loosens. The streak half of candidate (b) is now
measurable: 17 of 21 new-regime SLs had an earlier SL unbroken by a scratch, so
escalation to 60 min is the norm, not the exception (§4 says that is costing
wins).

## 6. Data hygiene — and a bug that was silently rewriting every conclusion

Three of the five analysis tools **crashed or lied** on this data drop. Fixed,
and worth remembering why they broke:

1. **SELL replay inversion (`tools/pathwalk_sims.py`) — the serious one.**
   `walk()` computed `hi_r = d*(h-e)/risk` and `lo_r = d*(l-e)/risk`, which for
   a BUY are the favourable and adverse bar extremes — and for a SELL are
   **exactly the reverse**. Every SELL TP/BE-arm test therefore required the
   *whole bar* to clear the level, so shorts under-detected both and mostly fell
   back to "use the actual outcome". The tool looked self-validating precisely
   because it was failing to a copy of reality. With 92 of 164 trades SELL, all
   sequence-aware numbers in the 09-10 PM review are suspect (their replay ≈
   actual is now explained). Fixed to `max/min` excursion form
   (`hi_r = max(d*(h-e), d*(l-e))/risk`), which is direction-agnostic and
   reproduces the engine: +0.30R simulated = 6W/21L/92BE, actual = 6W/21L/92BE.
   `phantom_trades.py` and `analyze_losers.py` already branched correctly.
2. **Ratchet-logged winners → ZeroDivisionError.** The engine writes the *live*
   stop at exit time, so `Stop_Loss == Entry_Price` appears on 98 rows: the 92
   BE scratches **plus 6 TP winners**. Tools keyed reconstruction on
   `Exit_Reason == "BE"` and divided by a zero risk. `analyze_losers.py`,
   `exit_sims.py` and `pathwalk_sims.py` now key on the geometry instead.
   Handoff §8's gotcha is updated accordingly.
3. **`analyze_losers.py` §4 was nonsense, now removed.** Its naive exit replay
   scored each trade inside the *actual* exit window — a 2-minute window can
   never reach a looser TP, so it printed "TP 1.50R → 0.6% win rate" against a
   real 19.4%. Any exit-geometry question must use a walk with an explicit
   horizon: `pathwalk_sims.py` (now has `horizon_min` rows) or
   `win_rate_report.py` §5b/§5c. Same class of trap as the 09-10
   `exit_sims.py` warning, one level up.
4. `check_data.py`: the "trade open across a price-log gap" check used strict
   `<`/`>` and missed #95 (it entered on the last bar before the gap). Bounds
   are now inclusive and it correctly warns. New checks: ratcheted-stop rows
   (info) and **>60-min holds (warn)**.
5. New read-only tool **`tools/win_rate_report.py`** — win-rate decomposition:
   CIs, era/side/day/hour splits, hold-time table, ratchet counterfactual,
   cascade-aware trigger grid, and the queued filters scored on current era vs
   all data. Add it to the post-drop routine.

Data notes from this drop: 98 rows with the ratcheted stop (6 non-BE, listed
above); **#95 held 49.1 h across the weekend gap** (entry Fri 09-11 20:57,
TP Sun 22:01 at reopen — the SL was at entry so P&L was +$3.38, but a live book

## 2. FOMC day — the gates held, and it was mostly not luck

**The Fed did not hold.** The FOMC **raised the target range 25 bps to
3.75–4.00%** on 2026-09-16, announced at **18:00 UTC** (2:00 pm ET), Chair
Warsh's presser at 18:30 UTC. Gold sold off hard on the hawkish surprise.

**Price action (broker demo GOLD feed):** pre-release close 4353.42 →
spike high **4367.01** (18:01) → crash low **4235.12** (19:11) = a
**−$118.3 (−2.7%)** round-trip in ~70 minutes. The 18:00 release bar ranged
$12.32, 18:01 $29.94, 18:02 $23.99 — against ~$4 mid-afternoon. ATR went
2.18 → 2.99 → **5.04** (18:02) and peaked 13.03 (18:14); it did not fall
back under 4.50 until 20:01.

**No trade was open at 18:00, and nothing entered from 17:19 to 20:36 UTC**
(117 min). A 2·ATR stop at ~2.04 ATR meant −$4.54/trade while the market was
making 1.8–5.2×-the-stop minutes — the bot was never on track to eat that.
And the coverage was **defense-in-depth, not pure luck**:

1. **SL cooldown (escalated) 17:28 → 18:28.** #218's 17:28 SL was the 3rd
   consecutive (after #216, #217), so the 60-min cooldown covered the whole
   pre-release fade (17:56–18:00, when ATR 1.51→1.85 was *still passable*)
   and the first 28 min of the waterfall. Absent this, an entry in the
   17:56–18:00 "re-rating" drift was possible and would have been untested.
2. **ATL (MAX_ATR 4.50) 18:02 → 20:01.** First bar with ATR > 4.50 was
   18:02, and it stayed above until 20:01 — a continuous wall across the
   whole cascade. This is the same generic news ceiling that was validated on
   09-10, doing exactly its job on its first real FOMC.
3. **RSI & trend gates sat out the fake-out 18:00 spike** (RSI 78–85 ~
   overbought; the long side was already closed by RSI ≤ 68) and the
   debounce never confirmed a momentum-chasing SELL in the first legs down.

So the real FOMC story is **not** "we dodged a bullet by luck" — the risk
layer (cooldown escalation specifically) is what kept 17:19–18:28 dark, and
ATL took the handoff to 20:01. That is genuinely reassuring, **with two
asterisks**:

- The one gap in the wall was **17:28 → 18:02 if cooldown hadn't been
  escalated** — i.e., the single most important consumer of this protection
  was the escalating-cooldown behaviour that candidate (b) (§4) proposes to
  weaken by letting BE reset the streak. FOMC day is now an explicit data
  point *in favour of* keeping escalation: it just saved real money.
- The only two losses after 17:00 (#218 17:19 −$2.51, #219 20:36 −$4.47)
  bracket the event — one each side — so the "no FOMC losses" result is
  partly that the book had already given the day back in the morning (§3).

**Un-blacked-out high-impact hours are real.** A 2:00 pm ET release is a
plannable calendar event and the bot has no scheduled-news gate (only fixed
windows). This instance happened not to need one; it is still an open gap,
now demonstrated cleanly against a known FOMC date. See §7.

## 1. BE ratchet: working as designed, P&L-neutral in a trend window

13 of 15 BE-era trades scratched (87% — far above the 27% replay prediction
of 12BE/45). Counterfactual bar-walk of the 13 scratches (original 2×/3×ATR
geometry reconstructed, 4h cap, SL-first tie-break): without BE they would
have been **5W/8L, +$4.08**. So the ratchet saved ~$30 of SLs
(#46,47,48,49,55,58,59,60) but killed 5 trend TPs (#50,52,53,54,56, ~$34) —
every one armed at +0.30R, wicked back to entry, then collapsed to TP.

Why the miss vs replay: the replay sample was BUY-heavy chop (losers never
recover); the live window was a SELL-heavy trend evening (pullbacks to entry
recover to TP). **BE helps chop and hurts trend** — P&L-neutral here,
variance-reducing everywhere (worst BE-era outcome −$5.06 vs −$6.47-class
full SLs). Supporting color: BE trades' early-MAE (first 3 min) is 0.37R —
they start like winners (0.28–0.45R), not losers (0.98R). The 2 BE-era SLs
(#51, #57) never reached +0.30R (MFE −0.48R / +0.09R): still "wrong
immediately", untouched by any exit tweak.

Verdict: **keep BE, don't tune on n=15.** Watch item for the next cycle: if
trend-TP-killing persists, study a risk-halving ratchet (SL → −0.5R instead
of entry) or a 0.50R trigger — on this window's 13 scratches neither trigger
level separates the 5 killed TPs from the 8 saved SLs (MFE overlaps: TPs
0.86–1.64R, SLs 0.31–1.37R), so the fix is stop *placement* after arming,
not the trigger.
